  "content": "# 🦖 ESP32-S3 Dino Runner Game\n\nA standalone, retro-style arcade game built on the **ESP32-S3** microcontroller. Features custom pixel graphics, responsive debounced button controls, delta-rendered LCD graphics to minimize screen flicker, dynamic tone-based audio effects, and status LED indicators.\n\n---\n\n## 📋 Features\n\n- **Non-blocking Visual & Audio Feedback:** Real-time jump sounds, victory jingles, and loss alerts via a piezo buzzer.\n- **Flawless LCD Delta-Rendering:** Only updates altered characters on the 1602 I2C LCD, eliminating display ghosting and I2C bus bottlenecking.\n- **Dedicated LED Indicators:** Win/Loss status indicators driven safely with current-limiting resistors.\n- **Hardware Noise Debouncing:** Software-filtered input checking for clean button presses.\n\n---\n\n## 🎮 How to Play\n\n1. **Power On:** Connect your ESP32-S3 to power via USB.\n2. **Start Screen:** The LCD displays the main title banner: `DINO RUNNER - Press Start!`. Press the jump button to begin.\n3. **Controls:**\n   - Press the **Jump Button** to make the Dino jump over oncoming small and large cacti.\n   - Each successfully cleared obstacle increments your score counter on the top row.\n4. **Game Over:** Hitting a cactus triggers the Game Over sequence, playing a loss melody and flashing the **Red LED**. Press the button to restart.\n5. **Winning the Game:** Reach a score of **250** to trigger the victory trophy sequence, accompanied by a fanfare jingle and a flashing **Green LED**.\n\n---\n\n## 🛠️ Hardware Requirements\n\n| Component | Quantity | Notes / Specifications |\n| :--- | :--- | :--- |\n| **ESP32-S3 Development Board** | 1 | Microcontroller core |\n| **1602 LCD Display with I2C Module** | 1 | Address `0x27` (or `0x3F`), 16x2 characters |\n| **Push Button** | 1 | Standard momentary switch for jump control |\n| **Piezo Buzzer** | 1 | Passive or Active buzzer for sound effects |\n| **Green LED** | 1 | Win indicator |\n| **Red LED** | 1 | Game Over indicator |\n| **Resistors (220Ω to 1kΩ)** | 2 | **Mandatory** for LED current-limiting protection |\n| **Breadboard & Jumper Wires** | Kit | Circuit interconnects |\n\n---\n\n## 🔌 Hardware Wiring & Pinout\n\n> ⚠️ **Important Safety Note:** Always use current-limiting resistors (220Ω–1kΩ) in series with the LEDs to prevent drawing excessive current from the GPIO pins.\n\n| Component | ESP32-S3 GPIO Pin | Notes |\n| :--- | :--- | :--- |\n| **LCD SDA** | **GPIO 8** | I2C Data line |\n| **LCD SCL** | **GPIO 9** | I2C Clock line |\n| **Jump Button** | **GPIO 17** | Connected to GND (Uses internal pull-up) |\n| **Piezo Buzzer** | **GPIO 4** | Positive terminal to pin, negative to GND |\n| **Green LED** | **GPIO 16** | Anode (`+`) through 220Ω resistor to GPIO 16 |\n| **Red LED** | **GPIO 15** | Anode (`+`) through 220Ω resistor to GPIO 15 |\n| **Common GND** | **GND** | All negative leads tied to ESP32 GND rail |\n\n---\n\n## 🔨 How to Build & Flash\n\n### 1. Hardware Assembly\n1. Connect the **I2C LCD** (`VCC` to `5V`, `GND` to `GND`, `SDA` to `GPIO 8`, `SCL` to `GPIO 9`).\n2. Wire the **Push Button** between `GPIO 17` and `GND`.\n3. Wire the **Buzzer** positive leg to `GPIO 4` and negative leg to `GND`.\n4. Connect the **Green LED** positive leg (long lead) through a 220Ω resistor to `GPIO 16` and the short lead to `GND`.\n5. Connect the **Red LED** positive leg (long lead) through a 220Ω resistor to `GPIO 15` and the short lead to `GND`.\n\n### 2. Software Setup\n1. Download and install [Arduino IDE](https://www.arduino.cc/en/software).\n2. Add ESP32 board support to Arduino IDE:\n   - Go to **File > Preferences** and add the following URL to *Additional Boards Manager URLs*:\n     `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/espressif/arduino-esp32/gh-pages/package_esp32_index.json`\n   - Go to **Tools > Board > Boards Manager...**, search for `esp32`, and click **Install**.\n3. Install the required I2C LCD library:\n   - Go to **Sketch > Include Library > Manage Libraries...**\n   - Search for **LiquidCrystal_I2C** (by Frank de Brabander) and install it.\n\n### 3. Uploading Code\n1. Select your board under **Tools > Board > ESP32 Arduino > ESP32S3 Dev Module**.\n2. Select your connected USB serial port under **Tools > Port**.\n3. Copy the project source code into your sketch.\n4. Click the **Upload** button.\n\n---\n\n## 📄 License\nThis project is open-source and available under the MIT License."
